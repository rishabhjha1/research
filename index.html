What I work on

Research focus

Four threads run through my work — all aimed at making deep learning usable in clinical settings, where data arrives sequentially, comes in multiple modalities, and needs to be explained.

Continual & federated

Learning without forgetting

2026 Preprint · arXiv

CADRE / FedCADRE — continual learning for medical imaging

A continual-learning framework that combines LoRA with self-scaling, similarity-aware EWC and an anchor-to-prior embedding penalty over a frozen BiomedCLIP backbone, so a model can learn a sequence of medical imaging tasks without catastrophic forgetting. FedCADRE extends the idea to the federated setting across sites.

SuperAger classification from structural MRI

A ViT-B/16 model with 2.5D multi-view preprocessing on ADNI and OASIS, benchmarked against four baselines (logistic regression, a simple CNN, DenseNet-40, ResNet-18) and paired with multi-method explainability — attention rollout, Grad-CAM, LIME, and grid-based regional analysis — plus an age-confound ablation and paired significance tests.

BioAlign-F — multimodal Alzheimer's classification

Fuses 3D brain MRI with CSF biomarkers using contrastive alignment (InfoNCE) and gated fusion, evaluated across five 3D backbones (ResNet-18, Simple ViT, Swin3D, MaxViT3D, FocalNet3D) on ADNI, with extensions toward tri-modal fusion (amyloid PET) and cross-cohort validation on OASIS-3 and AIBL.

2026 PhysioNet / CinC Challenge

Predicting cognitive impairment from polysomnography

An entry to the George B. Moody PhysioNet Challenge 2026: physiological feature engineering across seven signal groups combined with algorithmic and demographic features (162-dimensional) into a gradient-boosting classifier, run through the official challenge harness.

Background

I'm an MSc Computer Science researcher at the University of Victoria's Medical Computer Vision Lab, supervised by Prof. Ashery Mbilinyi. My work sits at the intersection of continual and multimodal learning and explainable AI, applied to neuroimaging and physiological signals.

Before Victoria I taught computer science as a lecturer in Nepal, and I've kept teaching as a TA and instructor at UVic. I'm now looking for a PhD where I can push continual and multimodal learning further on real clinical data.

Outside research I stay connected to the Nepali AI community and have a background in digital marketing and freelance work.
